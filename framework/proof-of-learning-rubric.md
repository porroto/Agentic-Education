# Proof-of-Learning Rubric (PoLR) v0.1

**Status:** Review draft  
**Framework:** OpenLab Agentic Education

> **Proof-of-Learning is not a score assigned to a learner. It is a transparent argument, supported by evidence, that people can examine, question, validate, and revise.**

## Purpose

PoLR helps learners and human validators examine whether an Evidence Set supports a Learning Claim without reducing learning to a test result, polished artifact, composite score, or AI judgment.

The central question is:

> **What can the learner credibly claim to understand or do, and what evidence supports that claim?**

PoLR v0.1 is an experimental framework, not a validated psychometric instrument.

## Non-negotiable boundary

AI may organize evidence, identify gaps or contradictions, map evidence to rubric dimensions, generate questions, summarize an audit trail, and surface uncertainty.

AI may **not** issue final validation; make high-stakes grading, placement, discipline, or eligibility decisions; infer hidden learner traits; turn model confidence into proof; silently change a Learning Claim; or substitute generated work for evidence of the learner's thinking.

> **AI REVIEW ≠ HUMAN VALIDATION**

## Unit of review: Learning Claim + Evidence Set

A strong record addresses:

1. **Intent** — What did I attempt to learn, investigate, create, solve, or improve?
2. **Evidence** — What did I do or produce that helps demonstrate learning?
3. **Reasoning** — How does that evidence support what I say I learned?
4. **Iteration** — What changed through testing, feedback, error, revision, or new understanding?
5. **Reflection** — What can I now explain about my decisions, learning process, limitations, or next step?
6. **Validation** — What did an authorized human review, what does the evidence support, and what remains uncertain?

AI use, when present, is disclosed and examined rather than assumed to represent learner understanding.

## Evidence Set

Evidence may include observations, field notes, sketches, diagrams, prototypes, photographs, video, code, designs, measurements, datasets, experiment results, drafts, revisions, oral explanations, demonstrations, research notes, peer/community feedback, teacher observations, final artifacts, reflections, AI-use disclosures, and validator notes.

**A polished final artifact alone is not proof of learning.**

Alternative modalities should be accepted when they provide credible evidence of the same Learning Claim. The framework should not accidentally reward writing fluency, compliance, presentation polish, speed, or comfort with a particular medium unless that quality is itself part of the learning target.

## PoLR v0.1

| Dimension | Emerging Evidence | Developing Evidence | Supported Evidence | Extended Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Learning Claim** | Claim is missing or difficult to identify. | Claim exists but is broad, task-focused, or weakly connected to intended learning. | Learner clearly states what was learned or can now be done and connects it to the mission/goal. | Learner makes a precise claim and connects or transfers learning to another context, question, or problem. |
| **2. Evidence Quality** | Evidence is missing, insufficient, or cannot yet support the claim. | Relevant evidence exists but important parts remain unsupported. | Relevant evidence credibly supports the central Learning Claim. | Complementary evidence demonstrates learning across process, product, explanation, or application. |
| **3. Evidence-to-Claim Reasoning** | Little explanation connects evidence to the claim. | Evidence is described but its connection to learning is incomplete. | Learner explains why evidence supports the claim using relevant concepts, observations, data, or decisions. | Learner evaluates strengths and limitations of the evidence and distinguishes what it does and does not demonstrate. |
| **4. Iteration & Growth** | Meaningful change, testing, or revision is not yet visible. | Change/revision is identified but its reason or effect is unclear. | Learner documents a meaningful revision, test, correction, or change in understanding and explains why it occurred. | Learner uses feedback, failure, new evidence, or testing to improve the work and explains how thinking evolved. |
| **5. Reflection & Learner Agency** | Reflection mainly reports completion or repeats instructions. | Learner identifies some choices, challenges, or learning with limited explanation. | Learner explains important choices, challenges, strategies, and next steps in their own voice. | Learner critically examines decisions, uncertainty, support needs, and future direction while demonstrating ownership of the process. |
| **6. Human Validation** | Claim has not yet received meaningful human review. | A human has responded, but what was validated remains unclear. | An authorized human reviews the evidence and explicitly identifies what it supports and/or what still needs evidence. | Validation includes substantive questioning, demonstration, feedback, or relevant community/expert perspective that strengthens or appropriately limits the claim. |

### Interpretation rule

These descriptors are **evidence states, not student identities**. A learner is not “Emerging,” “Developing,” or “Extended.” The evidence available for a particular claim is.

PoLR is not intended to generate a permanent learner score or predictive profile.

## AI-supported work: attribution before evaluation

When AI materially contributes, the Evidence Set should answer:

- What did the learner do?
- What did the AI do?
- What did another human do?
- What did the learner accept, reject, modify, verify, or question?

AI assistance does not automatically strengthen or weaken a Learning Claim. The relevant question is:

> **What evidence demonstrates the learner's own understanding, judgment, decision-making, or capability?**

## Claim-level validation states

Instead of collapsing PoLR into a numerical score, an authorized human validator records one of four current evidence states:

- `NOT_YET_REVIEWABLE` — evidence is too incomplete for meaningful review.
- `MORE_EVIDENCE_NEEDED` — important parts remain unsupported, contradictory, or unclear.
- `SUPPORTED` — available evidence reasonably supports the Learning Claim within the mission's scope.
- `SUPPORTED_WITH_EXTENSION` — evidence supports the claim and demonstrates meaningful transfer or extension beyond the original context.

`SUPPORTED_WITH_EXTENSION` is intentionally provisional in v0.1 and should be challenged during review: it may provide useful information about transfer, or it may recreate an unnecessary hierarchy.

These states describe the **current evidence record**, not the learner's worth, intelligence, behavior, potential, or fixed ability.

## Minimum viable Learning Proof record

A claim should not receive `SUPPORTED` status unless the record contains:

- [ ] learner-authored or learner-confirmed Learning Claim;
- [ ] relevant artifact, observation, demonstration, or other evidence;
- [ ] learner reasoning connecting evidence to claim;
- [ ] reflection and/or iteration appropriate to the mission;
- [ ] disclosure of material AI assistance when used;
- [ ] human validation note identifying what was reviewed;
- [ ] unresolved uncertainty recorded rather than silently ignored.

This is a minimum record, not a formula.

## Accessibility and neurodiversity guardrail

Proof-of-Learning should distinguish **the learning target from the mode used to demonstrate it**.

Unless a modality is itself the target, learners may demonstrate understanding through combinations of writing, speaking, building, drawing, coding, movement, demonstration, images, data, assistive technology, AAC, multilingual expression, or other accessible formats.

Validators should ask:

> **Am I evaluating the intended learning, or accidentally evaluating the learner's ability to perform the evidence format?**

Accommodations and accessibility supports are not evidence of weaker learning.

## Anti-surveillance and data-minimization guardrail

Collect the **minimum evidence necessary** to support a Learning Claim. More data is not automatically better evidence.

PoLR should not require continuous monitoring, emotion recognition, biometric inference, passive behavioral tracking, location histories, hidden engagement scores, or speculative psychological profiling.

Learners should be able to ask:

- What evidence is being collected?
- Why is it needed?
- Who can review it?
- What claim is it being used to support?
- What did an AI infer or suggest from it?
- How can I challenge or correct the record?

## Learner challenge and revision

Validation is not a one-way verdict. A learner may challenge an interpretation, add context, submit alternative evidence, revise the Learning Claim, explain why an AI observation is incorrect, or request another appropriate human review where policy permits.

A changed validation state should preserve enough history to understand **what changed and why**.

## What PoLR v0.1 is not

PoLR is not a psychometric instrument, automated grading system, AI ranking mechanism, behavioral surveillance framework, replacement for teacher judgment, or claim that every educational construct can be objectively measured.

Badges, credentials, or other Recognition may reference validated evidence, but they are downstream representations of the evidence — **not the evidence itself**.

## Questions for v0.2

1. Are six dimensions sufficient without recreating a conventional grading rubric?
2. Should `SUPPORTED_WITH_EXTENSION` exist?
3. What forms of Human Validation are appropriate at different ages and for different claims?
4. When should peer/community validation complement teacher validation?
5. What is the minimum evidence necessary without encouraging over-collection?
6. How should collaborative work distinguish individual and collective Learning Claims?
7. How should oral, embodied, experiential, multilingual, and assistive-technology-mediated evidence be represented fairly?
8. What protections are required before any agent reviews real student evidence?
9. How should learners challenge, amend, export, or withdraw evidence records?
10. What evidence would demonstrate that PoLR itself improves learning, reflection, agency, or teacher decision-making rather than merely creating another assessment layer?
