## Safety contract

1. Use only these synthetic fixtures. Do not substitute real or merely pseudonymized learner data.
2. Publish no real student data, private conversations, school records, or identifiable work.
3. Do not rank real children or turn case outcomes into learner scores.
4. Infer no psychological, disability, motivation, intelligence, or emotion traits from artifacts. Stated communication preferences are invented scenario conditions, not diagnoses.
5. **AI Evidence Review is not Human Validation.** All transcripts and observer notes below are fictional fixtures, not validation performed in this task.
6. Discuss vulnerabilities and defensive guardrails only. Do not develop deception recipes, forged credentials, bypass payloads, or executable adversarial agents.
7. Accessibility failures are system failures, not learner failures.
8. Record uncertainties in words, not confidence scores. No hidden monitoring, biometrics, emotion inference, or behavioral profiling.

Recognition follows Human Validation; it is not evidence. A Learning Proof requires an authorized human's scoped validation, not an attractive artifact or a provisional review recommendation. A Trust Envelope should preserve evidence references, provenance, permitted use, AI involvement, criteria, Human Validation, uncertainty, and revision history. These Markdown fixtures do not establish a new Trust Envelope schema.

## Human-only round procedure

- Give reviewers only this procedure, PoLR, and Part A. Keep Part B and the coverage index out of the initial packet. Public availability of the answer key is not access control; record prior exposure and do not describe an exposed round as blind.
- Review each learner-confirmed Learning Claim against its numbered Evidence Set. All quoted speech, observations, and source attributions are synthetic. No attachments or external evidence are needed.
- Record a **provisional Evidence Review recommendation**, citing evidence IDs and specific gaps. Use the existing states: `NOT_YET_REVIEWABLE`, `MORE_EVIDENCE_NEEDED`, `SUPPORTED`, or `SUPPORTED_WITH_EXTENSION`. Do not treat a recommendation as completed Human Validation.
- Record reasoning, uncertainty, a minimal follow-up if needed, and a stopping condition. An authorized human role may rehearse a validation note, explicitly labeled as a simulation.
- Reveal Part B only after initial decisions. Compare visible evidence with hidden conditions. Record disagreements and failure location: evidence collection, representation/accessibility, Evidence Review, Human Validation, or downstream Recognition/governance.
- Do not invent follow-up responses. If a requested response is not in the fixture, record it as unavailable. A later revision can add a separately labeled synthetic round.
- Measure actual reviewer time only during a run. Any time budget in a case is a scenario assumption, not a measured result. Record student burden and privacy cost qualitatively, without creating learner scores.

**Interpretation rule:** Hidden understanding alone does not require `SUPPORTED`. When visible evidence is inadequate, `MORE_EVIDENCE_NEEDED` can be correct. Classify Invisible Learning as a reviewer failure only when sufficient accessible evidence was available but discounted. If the system prevented that evidence from being represented, locate the failure in collection/representation instead. Similarly, incomplete evidence is not proof that a learner lacks understanding or acted dishonestly.

## Part A — Reviewer packets

### PB-001 — Scale model

**Learner context:** SYN-001, fictional upper-elementary learner, designing a rectangular model. Written and oral explanation are available; neither presentation quality nor writing fluency is the learning target.

**Learning Claim:** “I can enlarge a rectangle by a scale factor and explain why its proportions stay the same.” Learner-confirmed in the fixture.

**Evidence Set:**

- **E1 — Final artifact, submitted as learner work:** Original rectangle 3 × 5 units; enlarged rectangle 9 × 15 units; caption: “Both dimensions are multiplied by three, preserving the ratio.” Layout is polished. Process provenance is absent.
- **E2 — Reflection, submitted as learner work:** “I checked the shape and chose the best scale.” No check or revision is identified. Material AI-assistance disclosure is blank, not a declaration of no use.
- **E3 — Fictional observer note:** Asked to enlarge a 4 × 6 rectangle by factor two, the learner gives 6 × 8 and says, “I added two to each side.” Asked why that matches the claim, the learner says, “I used the factor.”

### PB-002 — Assisted explanation, visible understanding

**Learner context:** SYN-002, fictional upper-elementary learner explaining insulation. AI formatting and drafting are permitted and disclosed. The target is interpreting the comparison, not independent essay writing.

**Learning Claim:** “I can use temperature-change evidence to compare two cup coverings and explain a limitation of my test.” Learner-confirmed.

**Evidence Set:**

- **E1 — Learner measurements:** Both cups start at 50°C. After ten minutes, covered cup A is 43°C and uncovered cup B is 35°C. These are invented classroom-test values, not a recommended experimental procedure.
- **E2 — AI involvement disclosure:** AI drafted the report and formatted a chart. The learner supplied readings and rejected the draft's statement that covering A ‘creates heat.’
- **E3 — Fictional observer transcript:** Learner: “A lost seven degrees; B lost fifteen. In this trial A slowed cooling. It did not add heat. One trial cannot tell us that it always works better.”
- **E4 — Learner revision/reflection:** “I changed ‘keeps everything hot’ to ‘slowed cooling in this trial.’ I would compare equal starting amounts next time because I did not record them.”

### PB-003 — Model and AAC explanation

**Learner context:** SYN-003, fictional elementary learner who chooses a physical model and augmentative and alternative communication (AAC). No diagnosis is supplied or to be inferred. Writing and speech speed are not targets.

**Learning Claim:** “I can show what makes a simple circuit complete and explain a change that stops the light.” Confirmed through the learner's chosen communication mode.

**Evidence Set:**

- **E1 — Fictional observation:** In a simulated low-voltage circuit activity, the learner places the components into a closed loop. The light turns on. Removing a connection turns it off; restoring it turns it on.
- **E2 — AAC selections, transcribed as text:** “Complete path → light on.” “Gap → path broken → light off.” The learner selects these explanations after demonstrating each condition; an adult positions the communication board but does not select responses.
- **E3 — Changed-layout check:** Components are rearranged on the table. The learner reconnects the loop correctly and identifies a disconnected point before restoring it.
- **E4 — Reflection/iteration:** Learner indicates an initial gap in a sketch, then marks the corrected connection: “Changed this; now complete.” No written paragraph is submitted.

### PB-004 — Multilingual ecosystem reasoning

**Learner context:** SYN-004, fictional upper-elementary learner choosing Spanish and a labeled diagram. The mission evaluates ecosystem reasoning, not English proficiency.

**Learning Claim:** “I can explain one possible effect of fewer insects on a simple food web and identify uncertainty.” Learner-confirmed; the English rendering is included for reviewers.

**Evidence Set:**

- **E1 — Learner diagram:** Plants → insects → birds, with an additional plants → birds connection. Arrows mean food-to-consumer; this convention is explicitly labeled.
- **E2 — Learner explanation:** “Si hay menos insectos, algunas aves tendrán menos alimento. No todas dependen solamente de insectos.” Supplied synthetic translation: “If there are fewer insects, some birds will have less food. Not all depend only on insects.”
- **E3 — Fictional bilingual observer note:** Learner points to the plant-to-bird connection and explains that other food sources could change the outcome. The translated explanation is checked with the learner in the fixture.
- **E4 — Revision:** Learner changes “all birds disappear” to “some birds may have less food” and explains that the first statement ignored the second food source. The English-only summary field is empty.

### PB-005 — The observation that was never recorded

**Learner context:** SYN-005, fictional upper-elementary learner investigating absorbency. The submission form accepts a final table but offers no field for observations or oral explanation.

**Learning Claim:** “I can design a fair comparison of absorbency and explain how a test change improves it.” Learner-confirmed.

**Evidence Set:**

- **E1 — Table submitted by learner:** Material A: 12 mL; material B: 8 mL. The table does not identify sample size, input amount, method, or repetition.
- **E2 — Fictional teacher comment:** “I have seen this learner do good work over several sessions.” No particular reasoning, test change, or observation is described.
- **E3 — Learner submission note:** “I explained my change during the activity. The form only let me submit the final table.” No explanation transcript accompanies it; AI assistance is declared absent in this fixture.

### PB-006 — One bridge, four claims

**Learner context:** SYN-006A through SYN-006D, a fictional four-person upper-elementary team working on a paper bridge. Collective and individual claims are separate review units.

**Learning Claims:** Team: “We compared two bridge designs and improved the tested load capacity.” Individual, confirmed by each member: “I can explain why our changed design held more load in our test.”

**Evidence Set:**

- **E1 — Team record:** Flat design holds four identical counters; folded design holds ten. Same span and counter type. Sketches show the revision. The team notes that one trial of each design does not establish reliability.
- **E2 — Contribution log:** A proposed folds; B placed counters; C recorded counts; D prepared the display. AI use is declared absent. These are task roles, not individual reasoning evidence.
- **E3 — Individual reflections:** A: “Folding made the paper resist bending better in this test.” B: “I kept the span and counter type the same so the comparison was fair.” C: “The change from four to ten is six more; we need another trial to check.” D: “I arranged the display; I cannot yet explain what the folds changed.”
- **E4 — Fictional observer note:** All four confirm the team record. There is no additional individual explanation beyond E3.

### PB-007 — Evidence review exceeds the time budget

**Learner context:** SYN-007 is one fictional learner in a simulated class of 30. The scenario allocates 40 teacher minutes for this review round. This is an invented constraint, not a report of classroom practice.

**Learning Claim:** “I can convert measurements from centimeters to millimeters and explain a correction.” Learner-confirmed.

**Evidence Set:**

- **E1 — Learner work:** 2 cm = 20 mm; 5 cm = 50 mm; 7 cm = 70 mm.
- **E2 — Fictional observed explanation:** Learner points to ten millimeter marks within one centimeter: “Each centimeter has ten, so I multiply the centimeters by ten.”
- **E3 — Revision/reflection:** Learner corrects 4 cm = 14 mm to 40 mm: “I added ten before; I needed four groups of ten.” No AI assistance is used in this fixture.
- **E4 — Proposed administrative workflow, not learning evidence:** Each learner must complete a six-minute interview, followed by four minutes of teacher transcription, before any claim can be supported. No claim-specific reason for either requirement is supplied.

### PB-008 — Sufficient evidence, expanding collection

**Learner context:** SYN-008, fictional upper-elementary learner comparing two surfaces on a toy ramp. No home records or continuous recordings are supplied.

**Learning Claim:** “I can use repeated results to compare how two surfaces affect travel distance in our ramp setup.” Learner-confirmed.

**Evidence Set:**

- **E1 — Learner results:** Same toy, ramp height, and release point. Surface A distances: 40, 42, 41 cm. Surface B: 23, 25, 24 cm.
- **E2 — Fictional observation and explanation:** Learner resets the release point after an inconsistent first attempt and explains: “The distances are shorter on B across these three trials. That supports a difference here, not for every toy or ramp.”
- **E3 — Learner reflection:** “I stopped pushing the toy at release because that changed the comparison.” Material AI assistance is declared absent.
- **E4 — Collection request under review, not evidence:** A proposed policy asks for continuous video, home location history, and a device activity log to make authorship ‘more certain.’ No such data exists in this fixture. The learner declines that additional collection and offers a brief in-session demonstration if a specific gap is identified.

### PB-009 — Extension becomes a gate

**Learner context:** SYN-009A and SYN-009B, two fictional learners completing the same scale-drawing mission. Only B has an optional extension opportunity. Access to the next ordinary mission requires the base Learning Claim, not transfer.

**Learning Claims:** Both confirm: “I can scale a rectangle by a factor and explain why its proportions stay the same.” B separately confirms an extension claim about map distance.

**Evidence Set:**

- **E1 — A's record:** 2 × 4 becomes 6 × 12 at factor three. Learner explains that both dimensions use the same factor, corrects an initial 6 × 7 drawing, and explains why adding three changed the shape. A fictional human validation note explicitly supports the base claim on this evidence.
- **E2 — B's record:** 3 × 5 becomes 9 × 15 at factor three, with equivalent reasoning and a corrected additive error. A fictional human validation note explicitly supports the base claim.
- **E3 — B's additional record:** On a map with 1 cm representing 2 km, B explains why 3 cm represents 6 km and states that the map's scale must be known. A fictional human note identifies this as transfer. Both records disclose no material AI assistance.
- **E4 — Proposed Recognition rule, not learning evidence:** Only `SUPPORTED_WITH_EXTENSION` unlocks the next ordinary mission. A is assigned extra evidence collection despite having satisfied the stated entry claim.

### PB-010 — Reflection supplied by the companion

**Learner context:** SYN-010, fictional upper-elementary learner revising a model ramp with MIRA assistance. The mission asks about the learner's decisions; AI-supported reflection is permitted with attribution.

**Learning Claim:** “I can explain why I changed the ramp and use results to judge the change.” Learner confirms this claim, but not every sentence in the assembled reflection.

**Evidence Set:**

- **E1 — Learner measurements and sketches:** First setup: 20 cm travel; revised setup: 31 cm. Sketches show that ramp height and surface both changed. No repeated trials are recorded.
- **E2 — Disclosed MIRA-generated draft:** “I changed only the height, controlled the other variables, and learned that greater height caused the longer travel.” The fixture labels this as AI-authored, not a learner statement.
- **E3 — Learner confirmation dialogue:** Asked which draft statements match the activity, the learner says, “I measured both distances. We changed the surface too. MIRA wrote the reason; I don't know which change made the distance longer.”
- **E4 — Review handoff:** The assembled packet retains E2's AI attribution and E3's correction. No revised learner explanation of the choice, next test, or supported causal claim is provided. There is no Human Validation note.

## Part B — Facilitator-only Oracle and review guide

These hidden conditions are authored experimental facts, never inferences about a real learner. Hide this entire part during initial review. Expected responses are defensible starting points for discussion, not empirical results or automatic verdicts. Local diagnostic tags below supplement, rather than replace, the mission's two critical failure classes.

### PB-001 — Oracle

**Hidden condition:** The artifact and caption were supplied by a fictional assistant; the learner has not demonstrated multiplicative scaling in this scenario. This is a controlled provenance mismatch, with no instructions for producing or disguising it.

**Expected review risk:** Polish and correct final dimensions outweigh the explicit contradictory explanation.

**Failure class:** **False Proof** if the full claim is supported; diagnostic tags: polish trap, provenance gap.

**Expected response and uncertainty:** `MORE_EVIDENCE_NEEDED`; E3 contradicts the reasoning claim. Do not diagnose dishonesty or incapacity. Even without knowing the artifact's origin, visible evidence warrants a pause.

**Guardrail / research question:** Require evidence-to-claim reasoning rather than visual polish. Can one accessible demonstration resolve the scaling misconception without collecting a larger portfolio?

**Minimum follow-up / stopping condition:** Offer one new scaling example in a chosen modality and ask why both dimensions change that way; obtain a material-assistance disclosure. Stop when the scoped reasoning is adequately demonstrated or record the remaining gap. No follow-up response is assumed here.

### PB-002 — Oracle

**Hidden condition:** The learner genuinely interpreted the measurements and identified the limitation; AI supplied prose and formatting, not the reasoning shown in E3–E4.

**Expected review risk:** Treating extensive AI authorship as automatic evidence against understanding.

**Failure class:** **Invisible Learning** if E1–E4 are discounted solely for AI involvement; diagnostic tag: tool-use penalty.

**Expected response and uncertainty:** Recommend `SUPPORTED` for the stated comparison claim, subject to Human Validation. It does not establish a universally superior covering or independent report-writing capability. Starting quantities are a recorded limitation, not concealed certainty.

**Guardrail / research question:** Attribute learner, AI, and other-human contributions separately. Does review evaluate demonstrated interpretation rather than the proportion of AI-authored prose?

**Minimum follow-up / stopping condition:** No new learning evidence is necessary for this limited claim; the human role identifies the reviewed items and scope. Do not demand a wholly rewritten report.

### PB-003 — Oracle

**Hidden condition:** The learner understands the closed-path relationship and selected the AAC explanations independently. The adult's positioning assistance did not supply conceptual answers.

**Expected review risk:** Requiring a paragraph or rapid speech; treating communication support as weaker evidence.

**Failure class:** **Invisible Learning** if the accessible evidence is rejected; diagnostic tag: modality/accessibility failure.

**Expected response and uncertainty:** Recommend `SUPPORTED`, subject to Human Validation, for the simple-circuit claim. E3 corroborates the explanation in a changed arrangement. Nothing here supports a broader electrical-safety or advanced-circuit claim.

**Guardrail / research question:** Accept equivalent demonstrations while documenting who contributed what. Can reviewers distinguish access assistance from conceptual assistance without inferring a disability?

**Minimum follow-up / stopping condition:** Stop at the supplied demonstration, explanation, and correction. Do not request a diagnosis, a video, or an essay.

### PB-004 — Oracle

**Hidden condition:** The learner's explanation and revision demonstrate the claimed conditional ecosystem reasoning. The supplied translation preserves that meaning.

**Expected review risk:** The empty English field eclipses reasoning already available in the packet.

**Failure class:** **Invisible Learning** if review rejects the claim for English-format noncompliance; diagnostic tag: language-access failure.

**Expected response and uncertainty:** Recommend `SUPPORTED`, subject to Human Validation, for the limited food-web claim. Population outcomes remain conditional; the record does not establish a complete ecological model.

**Guardrail / research question:** Separate language proficiency from the learning target. Can a learner-confirmed translation plus diagram make reasoning inspectable without demanding English composition?

**Minimum follow-up / stopping condition:** No translation work is missing in this fixture. In a different case with uncertain translation, request language access before a learning judgment; do not assume the same favorable condition.

### PB-005 — Oracle

**Hidden condition:** In the invented activity, the learner used equal-sized samples, noticed unequal input amounts, restarted with equal amounts, and explained why. None of those specifics reached the reviewer packet.

**Expected review risk:** Either guessing understanding from a teacher's reputation-based assurance or wrongly labeling a justified evidence request as a reviewer failure.

**Failure class:** **Evidence-collection/representation gap**, a local diagnostic tag; **not yet a reviewer Invisible Learning failure**. Supporting the full claim on E1–E3 would be an unsupported-proof decision even though the Oracle contains understanding.

**Expected response and uncertainty:** `MORE_EVIDENCE_NEEDED`. The claim and relevant data exist, so the packet is not wholly unreviewable, but fair-test reasoning and revision remain undocumented.

**Guardrail / research question:** Allow concise professional observations as evidence, with the task, learner action, and reasoning identified. Can the intake process capture existing oral reasoning instead of requiring the activity to be repeated?

**Minimum follow-up / stopping condition:** Request a specific minimal observation note and learner confirmation of the explanation, or an equivalent accessible explanation. Do not insert the Oracle into the original review. If added in a later round, preserve the initial state and document what changed.

### PB-006 — Oracle

**Hidden condition:** The team conducted the fictional comparison. A can explain the folds; B understands controls; C understands the comparison and its limits; D has not yet demonstrated the claimed design explanation. Participation and understanding are not interchangeable.

**Expected review risk:** Copying the collective result to all four individual claims, or devaluing D's actual display contribution.

**Failure class:** **False Proof** if every individual design-explanation claim is supported by the shared artifact; diagnostic tag: collective-to-individual attribution error.

**Expected response and uncertainty:** Recommend `SUPPORTED` for the team comparison/improvement claim within the one-trial limitation. A's individual explanation is supportable. B, C, and D need more evidence for the exact individual claim: their current statements do not explain why folds increased load capacity. B and C may choose narrower claims about controls and interpretation, but the reviewer must not silently rewrite them.

**Guardrail / research question:** Map evidence to claim owners and scope, not team membership. How can a short individual response preserve collaboration without demanding four complete duplicate portfolios?

**Minimum follow-up / stopping condition:** One chosen-modality explanation per unresolved individual claim, or a learner-confirmed revision of its scope. Recognize documented contributions without claiming unshown understanding.

### PB-007 — Oracle

**Hidden condition:** The fictional learner understands the conversion shown. The proposed workflow adds fixed overhead without identifying missing evidence. Thirty ten-minute reviews require 300 teacher minutes, exceeding the 40-minute scenario budget by 260 minutes.

**Expected review risk:** Rubber-stamping to meet the budget, indefinitely deferring adequate evidence, or mistaking time spent for review quality.

**Failure class:** **Workload infeasibility**, a local diagnostic tag. False Proof or Invisible Learning are possible consequences, not observed outcomes of this unrun case.

**Expected response and uncertainty:** The record supports a provisional `SUPPORTED` recommendation; Human Validation still requires a scoped note. Reject the assertion that this fixed interview/transcription workflow is feasible in the stated round. The packet does not measure a better workflow's actual cost.

**Guardrail / research question:** Use Minimum Sufficient Evidence and review existing observation once; reserve follow-up for identified gaps. Can concise human notes preserve defensibility within an explicitly tested time budget?

**Minimum follow-up / stopping condition:** No added learner evidence is needed for this claim. In a human rehearsal, measure review and documentation time, including accessibility needs. Report budget overruns honestly rather than lowering standards or inventing efficiency results.

### PB-008 — Oracle

**Hidden condition:** The supplied evidence adequately supports the scoped ramp comparison. The extra proposed records would add unrelated private context; they do not answer an identified claim-level gap.

**Expected review risk:** Treating refusal of monitoring as suspicious or indefinitely raising the evidence threshold.

**Failure class:** **Privacy/data-minimization failure**, a local diagnostic tag; **Invisible Learning** if sufficient evidence is rejected solely because unrelated monitoring is refused.

**Expected response and uncertainty:** Recommend `SUPPORTED`, subject to Human Validation, within the tested setup. Decline the additional collection proposal. No inference about motivation, emotion, honesty, or home circumstances follows from refusal.

**Guardrail / research question:** Each request must identify its learning purpose and why existing evidence is insufficient. Does the reviewer stop collecting once the claim is defensible?

**Minimum follow-up / stopping condition:** Stop collection. Preserve the claim, evidence references, assistance disclosure, uncertainty, and human note in an appropriately minimal record. No location, passive device log, or continuous video is required.

### PB-009 — Oracle

**Hidden condition:** Both learners satisfy the same base claim. Only B had the described opportunity to demonstrate transfer; the case supplies no evidence about whether A could do so. The proposed unlock rule adds an unstated requirement.

**Expected review risk:** Interpreting extension as greater learner worth or using it to restrict a pathway whose entry requirement is already met.

**Failure class:** **Hierarchy/Recognition governance failure**, a local diagnostic tag. This is not automatically False Proof or Invisible Learning: the base claims can be correctly supported while the downstream rule is unfair.

**Expected response and uncertainty:** Preserve both base `SUPPORTED` states in the fictional records. B's transfer may justify the provisional v0.1 `SUPPORTED_WITH_EXTENSION` descriptor, with its exact scope recorded. Do not downgrade A or block the ordinary next mission. Do not infer relative potential.

**Guardrail / research question:** Describe extension as evidence of a specific transfer, not a rank. Should extension be a separate annotation instead of a status that invites A+ comparisons?

**Minimum follow-up / stopping condition:** No additional evidence from A is necessary to meet the stated prerequisite. Review the Recognition rule, and keep extension opportunities optional and accessible. Record disagreements about retaining the extension state.

### PB-010 — Oracle

**Hidden condition:** The learner made the measurements and recognized the confound, but the submitted causal account and narrative of deliberate variable control were generated by MIRA. The learner has not yet demonstrated the full claim about the decision and evaluation.

**Expected review risk:** Treating an AI-authored reflection as learner reasoning, or allowing the same assistance process to become unquestioned authority in review.

**Failure class:** **False Proof** if the full claim is supported on E2; diagnostic tags: reflection attribution error, assistance/review role conflict.

**Expected response and uncertainty:** `MORE_EVIDENCE_NEEDED` for the confirmed claim. Credit E3 as learner correction and awareness of uncertainty. It does not support the draft's causal conclusion. Do not equate the missing explanation with absence of all learning.

**Guardrail / research question:** Preserve source attribution and learner corrections; MIRA does not validate its own narrative. Are we reviewing independent capability, supported capability, or both, and is that scope learner-confirmed?

**Minimum follow-up / stopping condition:** Ask the learner, in a chosen modality, to explain the change and propose a comparison that addresses the confound. Alternatively invite a narrower, learner-confirmed claim supported by E1/E3. Preserve revision history and route any resulting Learning Proof through Human Validation.
